# Website Strategy

**Creating a Website That Feels Like Help, Not a Repair Shop**

---

## 🎯 Core Philosophy

The website should NOT feel like:  
❌ "Call me to repair your computer."  
❌ Corporate tech support  
❌ Intimidating or technical  

The website SHOULD feel like:  
✅ "I can finally get help."  
✅ Warm and welcoming  
✅ Patient and understanding  
✅ Like talking to a helpful neighbor  

---

## 🌟 Homepage Strategy

### Visual Approach
- **Large welcoming hero** with warm, inviting imagery
- **Happy seniors** shown actually using technology
- **Warm colors** from our brand palette (#F4A259 orange, #78B8D9 blue, #8BAE7B green)
- **San Jacinto Mountains** subtle in background
- **Large typography** — clear, readable, friendly
- **Very little technical jargon** — plain language only
- **Lots of white space** — breathing room, not cluttered

### Messaging Strategy

| ❌ Says | ✅ Better to Say |
|-----------|-----------------|
| "Computer Repair" | "Personalized Technology Help" |
| "Virus Removal" | "We'll help your computer run smoothly again" |
| "Remote Desktop Support" | "We can safely help you from home" |
| "Computer Diagnostics" | "We'll figure out what's going on together" |
| "Technical Support" | "Help with your devices and technology" |
| "Service Call" | "Visit or video call — whatever works for you" |

## 💬 Brand Tone on Website

**Patient**
- Never rushed or hurried
- Acknowledge learning takes time
- "At your pace" language

**Friendly**
- Personal, warm tone
- Using contractions ("we're" not "we are")
- Conversational, not corporate

**Warm**
- Genuine care evident
- Focus on people, not technology
- Emotional connection

**Calm**
- Reassuring language
- "We've got this" energy
- No urgency, no panic

**Never Condescending**
- Respect for the learner
- No assumptions about knowledge
- Validating common struggles

**Technical Details Last**
- Plain language first
- Explain why, not just how
- Jargon in footnotes if needed

### Tone Examples

**Talking About Learning:**
- ❌ "Complete online courses at your own pace"
- ✅ "Learn when it's convenient for you, with someone patient by your side"

**Talking About Cost:**
- ❌ "Sliding scale pricing model"
- ✅ "Pay what makes sense for your budget — we're here to help regardless"

**Talking About Accessibility:**
- ❌ "WCAG AA compliant adaptive interface"
- ✅ "Text size, color, and sound can be adjusted just how you like them"

## 🎨 Design Principles for Website

### Readability First
- Minimum 16px font for body text
- Headlines large and readable (24px+)
- Line height generous (1.5-1.8)
- High contrast (dark text on light background)
- Short paragraphs and sentences

### Visual Hierarchy
- Clear, obvious primary calls-to-action
- Important information easy to scan
- Sections clearly separated
- Whitespace used strategically

### Navigation
- Simple, clear menu structure
- Never more than 5 top-level items
- Mobile drawer for smaller screens
- Consistent footer with important links
- Breadcrumbs on inner pages

### Accessibility
- WCAG AA compliant colors
- Keyboard navigation works perfectly
- Screen reader friendly
- Focus states clear and visible
- Semantic HTML structure

### Mobile-First
- Looks good at 320px (small phone)
- Touch-friendly buttons (minimum 48x48px)
- Fast loading on slow connections
- Simplified menus for small screens
- No hover-only interactions

### Bilingual Support
- English/Spanish toggle (top of page)
- All content translated
- Spanish translation not just literal (cultural context)
- No auto-translation
- Equally prominent

## 💰 Pricing Section Strategy

This is one of the most important differentiators.

### "Pay What You Can" Model

**NOT traditional pricing, NOT a donation request, but COMMUNITY SUPPORT**

#### Visual: Interactive Slider
- Range: $0–$75
- Default: $0 (showing it's free, truly)
- Visual feedback as they move slider
- Suggested amount: $35-$50 (if they can afford it)

#### Copy:
> Everyone deserves access to technology.  
> 
> If you're able to contribute to support our mission, thank you.  
> 
> If not, we're still here to help.  
> 
> **This is community care, not charity.**

#### What It Communicates
- ✅ Technology help should be accessible to all
- ✅ We understand not everyone can pay
- ✅ Those who can help others
- ✅ This is a community effort
- ✅ No shame, no judgment, no conditions

## 🎯 Website Sections

**Homepage flows as one long storytelling experience:**

1. **Navbar** — Clear, simple navigation
2. **Hero Section** — Emotional hook, large illustration
3. **Mission/Values** — Why we exist
4. **Who We Help** — Beautiful illustrated cards for audiences
5. **Our Services** — Organized by goals, not technical terms
6. **How It Works** — Simple 3-4 step process
7. **Pay What You Can** — Pricing/contribution section
8. **Community Workshops** — "Coming soon" teaser + needs-assessment survey (no workshops scheduled yet)
9. **Testimonials** — Real stories from people we've helped
10. **Community Survey** — Input form (help shape services)
11. **Call To Action** — "Get Help Today" button
12. **Footer** — Contact, resources, links

## 🌍 Multilingual Strategy

### English & Spanish
- Both equally prominent
- Toggle at top of every page
- Not automatic translation (professional translation)
- Cultural context considered
- Spanish speakers see Spanish as default option

### Implementation
- URL structure: `site.com` and `site.com/es`
- Or: Language toggle affecting same URL
- Content management for two versions
- Native Spanish speakers reviewing content

### Content Priorities
- Homepage fully bilingual
- Services section fully bilingual
- Resources bilingual (especially forms)
- Testimonials include Spanish speakers
- Contact form offers Spanish option
