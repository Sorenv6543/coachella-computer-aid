# Community Technology Charter
### Coachella Valley — Working Foundation Document

*This is the "rebase" point. Everything — the website, the branding, the workshops, the repair work, the outreach — hangs off the mission below. If a future idea doesn't serve this mission, it doesn't belong. If it does, it fits naturally.*

---

## The Mission

**To make technology accessible, understandable, and empowering for every member of the Coachella Valley community — regardless of age, disability, language, income, or experience.**

Technology shouldn't create barriers. It should remove them. That's the whole job.

## The Vision

A Coachella Valley where every person has the confidence and knowledge to use technology independently and safely — whether they're 82 years old, new to the country, living with a disability, or simply overwhelmed. Everyone deserves patient help, without judgment.

## Why This Exists

The repair work was always a means to something bigger. The real shift happened when it became clear that the purpose isn't fixing machines — it's teaching confidence.

Your mother taught you patience. Not just patience, but *patience while teaching*. Helping people learn turned out to be the thing that gives the work meaning. That's a completely different business than computer repair.

What you're actually building:

- Helping someone make their first video call with their grandchildren
- Helping someone recognize and avoid a scam before it costs them
- Helping someone fill out a government form or apply for benefits
- Helping someone reconnect with family they'd lost touch with

Those are life-changing moments. That origin story should be the heart of the organization — on the website, in grant applications, in every conversation about what this is.

## Who We Serve

The homepage should never lead with "Computer Repair." It should lead with **"Who We Help."** Each group has a distinct set of real needs:

**Seniors** — smartphones, video calls, telehealth, email, photos, tablets, scam protection, smart TVs, streaming, password management.

**People with Disabilities** — adaptive technology, accessibility settings, voice assistants, screen readers, large text, device setup, remote support.

**Spanish-Speaking Families** — fully bilingual support, online forms, government and school portals, translation help, digital literacy, device setup.

**Veterans** — VA portals, telehealth, medical appointments, benefits websites, identity protection.

**Low-Income Families** — affordable repairs, donated computers, internet-access resources, device optimization, free workshops, community referrals.

**Anyone who needs help** — technology shouldn't require expertise. Everyone deserves patient help.

## What We Do

Organized by what the person is trying to accomplish — not by technical category.

**Learn** — one-on-one teaching, technology coaching, digital literacy, internet/computer/phone basics.

**Stay Safe** — scam and phishing prevention, identity-theft and fraud protection, password help, online safety and privacy.

**Stay Connected** — email, FaceTime, Zoom, WhatsApp, Facebook, photos, printing, video conferencing.

**Device Support** — laptop/desktop/printer setup and repair, Wi-Fi troubleshooting, smart TVs, phones, tablets, virus removal, backups, replacement hardware.

**Internet Access** — router and home-network setup, Wi-Fi optimization, affordable-internet programs, public internet resources.

**Remote Assistance** — secure remote support, family-assisted sessions, scheduled and recurring appointments.

## Community Outreach

This is one of the biggest opportunities, and the strategy is simple: **stop trying to serve everyone one-on-one, and go where people already gather.** The goal is to become known locally as the trusted "tech person" — through helping, never selling.

**Highest-leverage partners to approach first:**

- **Libraries** — free monthly classes and recurring "technology office hours." Easiest door to open, built-in audience, credibility by association.
- **Senior centers** — monthly presentations, tablet/phone help, scam-awareness sessions.
- **Nursing homes** — "technology connection days" helping residents video-chat with family and set up tablets. Emotionally powerful and highly shareable.
- **Churches** — office hours, volunteer events, community assistance.

**Next tier:**

- **Facebook groups** — a weekly helpful tip, no selling. This is the slow-build reputation engine that makes you "the local tech guy."
- **Schools** — technology nights, helping grandparents learn parent portals.
- **Homeless-services organizations** — recovering email accounts, applying for jobs, replacing IDs, navigating government sites, resume help.

**Recurring workshop formats that travel well:**

- Senior Tech Mondays (libraries, senior centers, retirement communities, churches)
- Scam Awareness (phishing, phone scams, AI scams, fake emails, Facebook and bank scams)
- Smartphone Basics (photos, contacts, texting, video calls, settings, accessibility)
- Computer Basics (mouse, keyboard, printing, email, downloads, internet)
- Technology for Caregivers (helping aging parents, remote support, medical tech, emergency contacts)
- Spanish Technology Workshops (fully bilingual — government services, school systems, healthcare portals)

## Marketing & Voice

The single guiding principle for everything written or said:

> The website — and every flyer, post, and conversation — should not feel like *"Call me to repair your computer."*
> It should feel like *"I can finally get help."*

**Tone rules:** patient, friendly, warm, calm. Never condescending. No technical jargon. When in doubt, describe the *outcome* the person wants, not the *technology* involved.

**Reframe the language everywhere:**

| Instead of… | Say… |
|---|---|
| Computer Repair | Personalized Technology Help |
| Virus Removal | We'll help you solve technology problems at your own pace |
| Tech Support | Patient help, on your schedule |

**The marketing rhythm** is reputation-first, not ad-first: show up consistently in the community and online with genuinely helpful content, and let trust compound. One weekly tip, one monthly workshop, one memorable nursing-home or senior-center visit does more than any paid campaign for this audience.

## Website Content

The site is the front door, and its whole job is to make an overwhelmed person feel relief, not to list services.

**Homepage:**

- Large, welcoming hero image — happy seniors, warm colors, the San Jacinto Mountains
- Large typography, lots of white space, high contrast, very little text
- Lead with **"Who We Help,"** not a service menu
- One clear, reassuring line of what this is and a simple way to reach out

**Design principles (already reflected in the work so far):** large fonts, simple navigation, high contrast, minimal jargon, warm and approachable illustrations. The friendly, senior-focused imagery and plain language we've built all point in the same direction — that *is* the identity that will resonate.

**Core pages:**

1. **Home** — hero + "Who We Help" + reassurance + contact
2. **Who We Help** — the six community segments, each with its real needs
3. **What We Do** — services grouped by goal (Learn / Stay Safe / Stay Connected / Device Support / Internet / Remote)
4. **Workshops** — the recurring formats and where to find them
5. **Our Story** — the "why," told honestly (the patience-while-teaching story)
6. **Pricing** — the pay-what-you-can philosophy, framed as community
7. **Contact** — dead simple, multiple ways to reach out, bilingual

---

## Pricing Philosophy

One of the most distinctive ideas, and it belongs on the site as-is: **Pay What You Can.**

A simple slider, roughly **$0–75**, with language like:

> Everyone deserves access to technology. If you're able to contribute, thank you. If not, we're still here to help.

The framing matters: this isn't charity. It's community.

## The Road Ahead

**Phase 1 — You.** Helping seniors, home visits, the website, Facebook, word of mouth.

**Phase 2 — Momentum.** Monthly workshops, volunteer helpers, donated computers, community partnerships (libraries, senior centers).

**Phase 3 — Structure.** 501(c)(3) status, grant funding, corporate sponsorships, digital-inclusion programs, a technology lending library, paid staff, a volunteer network.

**Phase 4 — The Community Technology Center.** A physical location where people can walk in for tech help, classes, computer and internet access, device repair, volunteer tutoring, digital-literacy programs, and community events.

## The One Thing That Changed

We originally framed this as a computer-repair business. That's too narrow. The repair work is real and important — but it's one service under a much larger mission.

**This is a community technology assistance organization.** Repair, Wi-Fi setup, smart-TV installation, remote support, workshops, scam prevention, digital literacy, bilingual assistance, and accessibility services all fit naturally under one unified purpose:

**Helping people use technology with confidence.**
